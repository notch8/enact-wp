# Editing the Enact site: a quick guide for the team

Everything on this site is editable in the browser with no code and no
developer. Log in at `/wp-admin` with your account.

## The two editors you will use

- **Posts and pages** (Posts > All Posts, Pages > All Pages): click a title to
  open the block editor. Type like a document; press `/` on an empty line to
  insert a block (image, heading, list, columns, quote, table, button).
  Update publishes immediately.
- **Site-wide things** (Appearance > Editor): header, footer, navigation menu,
  colours and fonts, and the layout of the news listing all live in the Site
  Editor. Edits here affect every page, and every change is previewed before
  you save.

## Common tasks

- **Publish a news post**: Posts > Add New Post. Give it a title, write, add
  images with `/image`. Set the category to News on the right-hand panel.
  Publish. It appears on the News page and the homepage automatically.
- **Edit a page**: Pages > All Pages > click the page. The homepage is the
  page called "Home"; each coloured band is a Group block you can click into.
- **Change the menu**: Appearance > Editor > Navigation. Add, remove, and
  reorder items by drag and drop; submenus are nested items.
- **Add someone to the team page**: open the Team page, click an existing
  person's column, and use the three-dot menu > Duplicate, then swap the
  photo and text. Remove a person by deleting their Column block.
- **Swap an image**: click the image, then "Replace" in its toolbar. Please
  fill in the alt text field (accessibility) when uploading anything new.

## Roles

- **Administrators** can do all of the above plus manage users and settings.
- **Editors** can write and publish posts and pages but not change site
  structure or users. Day-to-day news publishing works well at this level.

## Things that revert if changed carelessly

Nothing. There is no page builder, no custom theme code, and no plugin stack:
this is stock WordPress with the bundled Twenty Twenty-Five theme, so every
screen matches WordPress's own documentation at https://wordpress.org/documentation/.
If an edit goes wrong, each post/page keeps revisions (right-hand panel >
Revisions) you can roll back to.
