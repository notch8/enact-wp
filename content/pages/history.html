Enact brings together the knowledge and expertise of two Practice Research (PR) projects funded by UK Research and Innovation's (UKRI) Arts and Humanities Research Council under its Scoping Future Data Services for the Arts and Humanities call, completed in 2022: Practice Research Voices and Sustaining Practice Assets for Research, Knowledge, Learning and Education (SPARKLE).

Since these projects concluded in July 2022, the two teams have merged together, reflecting on and refining our approach, with key principles agreed such as incremental development and working in co-design with all Enact communities, who include academic and discipline practice researchers and related experts in fields such as repositories, research data, open standards and research management.

Ongoing investment (by AHRC and the University of Westminster) and the time of the Enact team has enabled:

  • Documentation of the PR Voices metadata schema
  • Development of technical requirements to underpin a procurement process to appoint the Enact technology partner
  • Building our engagement with global communities including co-creating the Research Data Alliance Infrastructure for Non-Traditional Research Outputs and Processes Interest Group

Practice Research Voices (PR Voices)

PR Voices, led by library and research management domain experts, working in close collaboration with academic discipline experts, reviewed existing open-source software and standards and began building the intersections PR community. It built on Westminster's pioneering PR repository development work and tested findings from the PRAG-UK reports.

Sustaining Practice Assets for Research, Knowledge, Learning and Engagement (SPARKLE)

SPARKLE worked to build on the PRAG-UK reports to scope what it means to provide a heterogeneous and extensible infrastructure for practice research that befits the multi-faceted, multi-disciplinary, multi-format nature of practice research, and reducing reliance on standardised asset-types.

SPARKLE collated data from workshops and interviews across nine stakeholder categories – including practice researchers and library/repository experts – on the challenges and needs identified in creating an infrastructure for FAIR access to practice research.
